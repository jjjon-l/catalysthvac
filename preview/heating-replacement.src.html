Repair or Replace?

Replacement Should Solve a Bigger Problem Than a Single Failed Part

A heating system doesn't need replacing just because it needs a repair. Replacement becomes more relevant when the current failure is part of a broader pattern, or when you want to change what the HVAC system can deliver.

Decision signals

  • Recurring or increasingly expensive repairs
  • Declining reliability
  • Persistent comfort or airflow problems
  • Rising operating costs or declining efficiency
  • A major component failure on equipment already in poor overall condition
  • Changes to the home that affect heating and cooling needs
  • Plans to change system type or improve year-round performance

ENERGY STAR identifies frequent repairs, rising energy use, comfort problems and equipment age as reasons to consider replacement. It lists furnaces over 15 years and heat pumps over 10 years as age indicators, but those are decision signals, not automatic rules. Actual condition and economics still matter.

Heating System Options

The Replacement Path Depends on the Home and the Existing HVAC System

Greater Houston homeowners may have several heating paths depending on the property, existing equipment, fuel source, duct system and how heating and cooling are configured.

{{icon:heating-replacement}} Option

Furnace

A forced-air furnace creates heat and distributes it through the home's ducts. A replacement decision may involve fuel type, efficiency, airflow, controls, venting and combustion requirements where applicable, and compatibility with the rest of the HVAC system.

{{icon:heat-pump}} Option

Heat Pump

An air-source heat pump provides both heating and cooling by moving heat rather than relying on combustion. Because it serves both seasons, choosing one is a whole-system decision, not a heating-equipment swap.

{{icon:dual-fuel}} Option

Dual-Fuel / Hybrid

A dual-fuel system pairs a heat pump with a furnace so the system can use different heat sources under different conditions. ENERGY STAR recognizes this as an option where a home has or is considering both.

{{icon:ac-replacement}} Option

Electric Furnace and Package Units

Many Houston homes heat with an electric furnace or air handler, or with a package unit that combines heating and cooling in one cabinet. Catalyst replaces both.

Catalyst installs Daikin furnaces, heat pumps and package units in gas, electric and dual-fuel configurations.

Right-Sizing the System

A New System Should Be Selected for the Home It Is Going Into

Replacing equipment with the same nominal size doesn't by itself show the new system is correctly sized. The home's actual heating and cooling loads depend on square footage, insulation, windows, orientation, air leakage, duct conditions, additions and other characteristics.

ENERGY STAR recommends that contractors verify sizing with a Manual J load calculation rather than rules of thumb, and points to ACCA design standards for system selection and matching.

For a heat pump this matters on both sides: the equipment has to meet the home's cooling demand while also delivering appropriate heating for the chosen configuration.

Catalyst sizes every heating replacement with a Manual J load calculation and selects equipment with Manual S, so the system fits the house rather than the old unit's label.

Heat Pumps in Houston

A Heat Pump Can Change the Heating and Cooling Conversation

An air-source heat pump is a year-round system: it cools in warm weather and reverses to heat when temperatures fall. That makes it important to evaluate it as a matched heating-and-cooling decision rather than comparing it only with a furnace.

Efficiency terms: residential heat pumps are rated with SEER2 for cooling and HSPF2 for heating. Higher ratings mean greater rated seasonal efficiency, but efficiency alone doesn't guarantee lower bills or better comfort; sizing, installation, ducts, controls, usage and utility costs all affect real results.

Useful questions if you're considering one

  • What are the home's heating and cooling loads?
  • Is the existing duct system right for the new equipment?
  • What capacity and efficiency level fit the home and budget?
  • Will the home use a heat pump alone or a dual-fuel setup?
  • How will the system perform during Houston's colder weather events?
  • What controls and thermostat configuration are needed?

The Installation Matters

Good Equipment Can't Correct a Poor Installation

A replacement system's performance depends on more than the equipment label. Sizing, component matching, airflow, duct conditions, controls, refrigerant charge for heat pumps and installation details all affect comfort, efficiency and equipment life.

A complete replacement conversation accounts for the system around the equipment, not just the box coming out and the box going in. Considerations can include:

  • Correct sizing and matching
  • Duct and airflow conditions
  • Electrical requirements
  • Thermostat and control compatibility
  • Refrigerant piping and charge for heat pumps
  • Condensate management where applicable
  • Furnace venting and combustion requirements where applicable
  • Startup, testing and commissioning
  • Access for future service and maintenance

ENERGY STAR's quality-installation guidance emphasizes right-sizing, airflow, refrigerant charge and duct evaluation. DOE warns that oversizing, improper charging and leaky ducts can reduce performance and shorten equipment life.

On every install, Catalyst checks airflow and static pressure, verifies refrigerant charge on heat pumps, tests gas pressure and venting on furnaces, and documents the startup readings.

What Are You Trying to Improve?

Replacement Can Be About More Than Restoring Heat

You may be replacing a heating system because the old one failed, but a new system is also a chance to address broader goals: more consistent comfort, year-round efficiency, less noise, better humidity and airflow, or a change in how the home is heated and cooled.

The right option depends on your priorities and the home. A higher efficiency rating can reduce energy use under comparable conditions, but actual savings vary with weather, thermostat settings, construction, sizing, installation quality, maintenance and energy prices.

For heat pumps, both SEER2 and HSPF2 belong in the conversation because the same system cools and heats.

Heating Replacement Questions

How do I know whether to repair or replace my heating system?
There's no single rule. Consider the current failure, repair cost, overall condition, repair history, comfort, efficiency, equipment age and what you expect from the system going forward. If the problem may be repairable and you're unsure, a repair evaluation is a reasonable starting point.
How old is too old for a furnace?
Age is one factor, not an automatic cutoff. ENERGY STAR identifies furnaces over 15 years old as a reason to consider replacement, especially when repairs, energy use or comfort problems are also increasing.
How old is too old for a heat pump?
ENERGY STAR identifies heat pumps over 10 years old as one reason to consider replacement, but condition, performance, repair history and replacement economics still matter.
Should I replace my furnace with a heat pump?
A heat pump provides both heating and cooling and may be the right path for some homes. It depends on the home's loads, existing configuration, ducts, electrical and fuel considerations, equipment options and your priorities. Some homes use a dual-fuel system that pairs a heat pump with a furnace.
Why does HVAC sizing matter when replacing a system?
Equipment that's too large or too small can create comfort and performance problems. ENERGY STAR recommends sizing from the home's actual requirements with a Manual J load calculation rather than rules of thumb or copying the existing equipment size.
What do SEER2 and HSPF2 mean?
SEER2 is a seasonal cooling-efficiency rating and HSPF2 a seasonal heating-efficiency rating used for heat pumps. They help compare rated efficiency but don't by themselves predict a specific home's utility bill.
