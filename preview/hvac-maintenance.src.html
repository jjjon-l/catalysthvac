Preventive HVAC Care

Maintenance Is About Knowing How the System Is Running Before You Need a Repair

HVAC maintenance isn't a promise that equipment will never fail. It's a structured opportunity to inspect important components, confirm basic operation, identify conditions that may need attention and keep routine care from being left to chance.

That's a better rhythm than waiting until the system stops cooling or heating before anyone looks at it.

ENERGY STAR recommends annual pre-season professional checkups and lists thermostat settings, electrical connections, condensate drainage, system controls, coils, refrigerant level and blower components as common parts of a maintenance check.

Catalyst's own visit covers each of these items, plus an outdoor coil rinse, a condensate drain flush and a written summary of what the technician found.

Built for the Houston Climate

Greater Houston's long cooling season makes the condition of the AC system especially important. Filters, coils, drainage, airflow and outdoor equipment all affect how the system runs, and routine maintenance creates a scheduled point to inspect them before the hottest part of the season.

Between Service Visits

The Filter Is One of the Simplest Things to Keep an Eye On

A dirty filter can restrict airflow and make the system work harder. ENERGY STAR recommends checking the filter monthly, especially during heavy use, and replacing or cleaning it when dirty. The right filter type and schedule depend on the system, the filter and conditions in the home.

Other simple steps: keep the outdoor unit reasonably clear of leaves and debris, and pay attention to changes in comfort, airflow, noise, cycling or drainage.

When to Schedule

Plan Maintenance Before the System Faces Its Heaviest Seasonal Load

ENERGY STAR recommends professional pre-season checkups: cooling before summer and heating before winter. For Houston homeowners, cooling preparation deserves particular attention because of the length and intensity of the cooling season.

Catalyst recommends two visits a year: a cooling check in spring and a heating check in fall.

From Inspection to Next Step

Finding an Issue Doesn't Automatically Mean Replacing the System

If an inspection finds something that needs attention, the next step should match the problem. Some findings call for a repair. Others just need monitoring or routine care. If the system has larger reliability, performance or repair-cost concerns, replacement can be evaluated separately.

The purpose of maintenance is better visibility into the system, not a manufactured replacement decision.

HVAC Maintenance Questions

How often should HVAC maintenance be done?
ENERGY STAR recommends annual pre-season professional checkups, with cooling service before summer and heating service before winter. Catalyst recommends a spring cooling visit and a fall heating visit.
Is HVAC maintenance the same as AC repair?
No. Maintenance is routine inspection and care. If your system already has an active cooling or operating problem, request AC Repair so the issue can be diagnosed directly.
What should be checked during HVAC maintenance?
A professional maintenance visit commonly looks at controls, electrical connections, condensate drainage, coils, airflow components, filter condition and cooling or heating operation. A Catalyst visit also includes a coil rinse, a drain flush and a written summary of what was found.
How often should I change my HVAC filter?
Check it regularly. ENERGY STAR recommends checking monthly, particularly in heavy-use seasons, and replacing or cleaning it when dirty. The right interval varies with the filter, the system and conditions in the home.
Can maintenance guarantee my AC won't break down?
No maintenance program can guarantee equipment will never fail. Routine maintenance gives you a chance to spot conditions that deserve attention and keeps regular care on a schedule.
