Heating Repair

Heating Repair for Greater Houston Homes

If your home isn't heating properly, the system won't start, airflow feels wrong or the equipment is behaving differently than it should, Catalyst can evaluate the problem and determine the right next step.

The goal is to find what's actually wrong before deciding whether the answer is a repair, a system adjustment, maintenance or a replacement conversation.

What Is Your Heating System Doing?

Start With the Problem You Are Experiencing

Heating problems can start in the equipment, controls, airflow, electrical components, combustion system or, in heat-pump systems, the refrigeration cycle. The symptom is a starting point, not the diagnosis.

No Heat

The thermostat is calling for heat, but the home isn't warming or the system doesn't seem to be operating correctly.

System Won't Start

A thermostat, electrical, control, safety or equipment issue may stop the heating cycle from starting normally.

System Runs but the House Stays Cold

Poor heating performance can involve the equipment itself, airflow, duct conditions, controls, system sizing or other factors that need to be evaluated together.

Uneven Heating

Rooms that stay noticeably warmer or colder than others can point to airflow, duct, balancing, insulation or equipment-performance issues rather than a single failed part.

Short Cycling or Frequent Starts and Stops

Repeated cycling can have several causes. The operating pattern and system conditions should be diagnosed before assuming the equipment needs replacing.

Unusual Sounds or Odors

Changes in sound or smell deserve attention, especially during startup or operation. Stop using the equipment and get appropriate help if you suspect a safety issue.

Heat Pump Isn't Heating Normally

Heat pumps use the refrigeration system for both cooling and heating. Heating problems can involve controls, airflow, refrigerant-side components, auxiliary heat or other system conditions.

When Safety Comes First

Some Heating Problems Shouldn't Wait for Routine Troubleshooting

Heating equipment can involve electricity, combustion, gas and exhaust. If you smell gas, suspect carbon monoxide, see smoke or fire, or believe the equipment is operating unsafely, leave the immediate hazard area and follow the appropriate emergency or utility guidance before treating it as a normal HVAC service request.

Heating Diagnostics

A Repair Starts With Finding the Cause

A useful heating service call moves from the symptom to the system condition causing it. The exact diagnostic sequence depends on the equipment and what the technician observes.

You should leave the diagnostic conversation understanding what was found and which path is recommended.

After the Diagnosis

The Next Step Should Match the Condition of the System

Once the cause is understood, the conversation can move into one of several paths.

Path 1

Repair

When a specific failure can be corrected and the broader condition of the system still supports repair, the next step may be straightforward service work.

Path 2

Adjustment, Airflow or Control Correction

Not every comfort complaint is a major equipment failure. Some involve controls, airflow, filters, duct conditions or another part of the overall system.

Path 3

Maintenance

If the equipment runs but has deferred maintenance or developing issues, a maintenance path may make sense after the immediate problem is handled.

Path 4

Replacement Evaluation

If the system has recurring failures, significant component problems, poor performance or repair economics that no longer make sense, replacement can be evaluated as a separate decision.

Repair or Replace?

A Heating Problem Doesn't Automatically Mean You Need a New System

Age matters, but it isn't the only factor. A better decision weighs the specific failure, repair cost, overall condition, repair history, comfort, efficiency and what you expect from the system going forward.

If repair still makes sense, this page supports that decision. If replacement deserves a look, Catalyst moves you into a separate replacement conversation rather than forcing the decision inside a repair call.

ENERGY STAR identifies frequent repairs, rising energy use and persistent comfort problems as signs replacement may be worth considering. It also uses age as one indicator, including furnaces over 15 years and heat pumps over 10 years, but those are decision signals, not automatic replacement rules.

Heating System Types

The Repair Approach Depends on the Equipment in the Home

Furnace

A forced-air furnace creates heat and distributes it through the home's ducts. Service may involve the ignition and combustion system, controls, blower, electrical components, airflow or distribution, depending on the symptom and furnace type.

Heat Pump

A heat pump moves heat rather than creating it through combustion and can provide both heating and cooling. Because the same system runs year-round, a heating complaint may involve components that also affect cooling.

Dual-Fuel / Hybrid

Some homes pair a heat pump with a furnace and use different heat sources under different conditions, which makes controls and system integration part of the diagnosis.

Heating in Greater Houston

Houston's Heating Season Is Different, but the System Still Has to Work When You Need It

Greater Houston is cooling-dominated, so a home's heating equipment may sit unused for long stretches before a cold front creates sudden demand. That makes reliable startup, controls, airflow and pre-season maintenance especially relevant even though heating isn't the system's main annual workload.

Before the Next Cold Front

Heating Maintenance Can Find Problems Before the First Real Demand

ENERGY STAR recommends annual pre-season HVAC checkups, with heating equipment typically checked before winter. A heating maintenance visit can evaluate operation before the system is suddenly needed after months of limited use. If you've just had a repair, it's also a natural point to set up an ongoing maintenance schedule rather than waiting for the next failure.

Heating Repair Questions

Why is my heater running but not warming the house?
Several conditions can cause that, including equipment performance, airflow, duct, thermostat or control, or system-capacity issues. A technician needs to evaluate the system before the cause can be identified reliably.
When should I repair my heating system instead of replacing it?
Repair can still be reasonable when the failure is isolated, the equipment is otherwise in serviceable condition, and the cost and expected reliability make sense. Replacement becomes more relevant when repairs are frequent, major components are failing, comfort or performance is declining, or the economics no longer support continued repair. Explore heating replacement.
How old is too old for a furnace?
There's no single age at which every furnace should be replaced. ENERGY STAR lists furnaces over 15 years old as one reason to consider replacement, but condition, repair history, efficiency, comfort and repair cost matter too.
Do heat pumps work for heating as well as cooling?
Yes. Air-source heat pumps provide both heating and cooling by moving heat between indoors and outdoors. The right equipment and configuration depend on the property, the existing HVAC system and installation requirements.
Should I have my heating system checked before winter?
ENERGY STAR recommends annual pre-season checkups and suggests checking the heating system in the fall. The schedule should also follow the equipment manufacturer's requirements. HVAC maintenance.
