Project Situations

When the Work Is Larger Than a Routine Service Call

A property may be evaluating replacement of failing or aging equipment, a multi-unit upgrade, changes to system capacity or controls, or work that needs careful scheduling around tenants, occupants and other contractors. Each project needs a practical definition of the technical scope, the building constraints and the decisions to make before work begins.

The right first question isn't just "What equipment do we buy?" but "What must this project accomplish, and what conditions shape the work?"

The Project Conversation

From Initial Scope to an Executable Plan

  1. Understand the Property and Need

    Facility type, location, affected systems, the present issue, intended outcome and any urgency.

  2. Establish the Technical Scope

    Review available system information, equipment and constraints; note what needs further inspection or design input.

  3. Identify Dependencies

    Access, permitted work windows, electrical, structural or other trade dependencies, owner approvals, and tenant or occupant considerations.

  4. Clarify Responsibilities

    Separate Catalyst's work from tasks belonging to the owner, engineer, other trades or the jurisdiction.

  5. Define the Proposal and Next Step

    Scope, exclusions, schedule assumptions and the documentation a final commercial proposal would include.

Occupied Buildings

Plan Around the Building, Not Just the Equipment

Office, retail, multifamily, senior living and other facilities are often occupied while HVAC decisions are made. A useful project discussion identifies which spaces rely on the equipment, when access is possible, how outages or temporary disruptions would be handled and who needs updates.

Catalyst has completed projects in offices, retail space, multifamily properties and senior living communities.

Equipment Decision

Make the Equipment Decision in the Context of the Project

A replacement decision should account for the existing equipment, actual load and building needs, distribution and controls, compatibility, energy goals, site limits and installation requirements.

Differences in proposal price can reflect differences in equipment, contractor scope, logistics and commissioning, so it helps to compare what's included, not just equipment labels.

  • Existing equipment
  • Actual load and building needs
  • Distribution and controls
  • Compatibility and energy goals
  • Site limits and installation requirements

HVAC Project Questions

Can I discuss a project before all equipment specifications are final?
Yes. An initial inquiry can describe the property, existing problem, goals and available information. A definitive proposal may need further technical review.
What should a project inquiry include?
Property location, facility type, point of contact, affected equipment, desired scope, constraints and anticipated timing.
Can work be scheduled around building operations?
It depends on the project and property. Operating constraints are documented before any scheduling commitment.
Does Catalyst coordinate with other contractors or engineering teams?
Yes. Catalyst coordinates with the owner's engineer, electricians and other trades whose work affects the HVAC scope, and keeps the facility contact updated on schedule and changes.
Does Catalyst offer service after project completion?
Yes. Installed equipment can move onto a Catalyst planned-maintenance agreement, and warranty and service questions come straight to Catalyst.
